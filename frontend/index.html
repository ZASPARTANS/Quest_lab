        How to read the blocks

        Each block is 6 hours long and starts at the time shown. The word and the drops are the rain level of the wetter of the two models; "models differ" means they are in different levels. A tick means the block fits what you want to do. Faded blocks are over or outside your hours. Press a block to see it on the map.

        What this page does

        Rain plan shows when rain is expected at a place over the next 10 days, and picks the times when the rain stays under the limit of what you want to do.

        How to use it

        1. Where. Press "Use my location", search for a town, or click the map.
        2. What. Choose what you want to do, for example "Drying laundry" or "Commute". Each activity has its own rain limit; with "Custom" you set the limit yourself.
        3. Best times. The page lists the 6-hour blocks that stay under that limit. "Day by day" shows every block; tap one to see it on the map. The time bar on the map moves the rain map through the days.

        The two forecasts, and which to pick

        The data is free and comes from ECMWF, the European weather centre. It has two forecasts, and a new run of each arrives about every 12 hours.

        • IFS is ECMWF's main forecast, calculated from the physics of the atmosphere.
        • AIFS is ECMWF's machine-learning forecast, trained on past weather.
        • Both models (agreement) shows where they agree: bright blue means both say rain, dim blue means only one does.

        The plan on the left always uses both and takes the wetter of the two, so it leans careful. On the map, start with "Both models (agreement)"; choose IFS or AIFS to see one model's own rain. Neither is right every time: where the page says "models differ", treat the forecast as less certain.
